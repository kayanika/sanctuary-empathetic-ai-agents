SYNTHETIC / PARAPHRASED CLINICAL CRITERIA — NOT VERBATIM DSM-5
================================================================
This file contains structurally faithful, paraphrased diagnostic criteria written
for development and testing of the TAO retrieval pipeline. It is NOT the verbatim
DSM-5 text (which is copyrighted by the American Psychiatric Association) and must
not be cited as an authoritative clinical source. Numeric thresholds (symptom counts,
durations) reflect publicly known diagnostic facts. To use licensed real criteria,
place them at data/dsm5_real.txt (gitignored) and re-run the knowledge base builder.

## Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

### Core definition
A major depressive episode is characterized by five or more of the nine symptoms
listed below, present during the same two-week period and representing a change from
prior functioning. At least one of the five must be either (1) depressed mood or
(2) markedly diminished interest or pleasure.

### Symptom criteria (five or more required)
1. Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day (sad, empty, or hopeless).
2. Markedly diminished interest or pleasure in nearly all activities (anhedonia).
3. Significant unintentional weight change or appetite change nearly every day.
4. Insomnia or hypersomnia nearly every day.
5. Psychomotor agitation or retardation observable by others.
6. Fatigue or loss of energy nearly every day.
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive or inappropriate guilt.
8. Diminished ability to think or concentrate, or indecisiveness.
9. Recurrent thoughts of death, recurrent suicidal ideation, or a suicide attempt.

### Duration requirement
Symptoms must persist for at least two weeks (14 days). An episode shorter than two
weeks does not meet the threshold for a major depressive episode.

### Distress and exclusion criteria
The symptoms must cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social,
occupational, or other important areas of functioning. The episode must not be
attributable to the physiological effects of a substance or another medical condition,
and must not be better explained by a psychotic disorder. There must never have been a
manic or hypomanic episode (which would indicate a bipolar disorder instead).

## Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)

### Criterion A — Exposure to trauma
Exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence by one or
more of: directly experiencing the event; witnessing it in person; learning that it
happened to a close family member or friend; or experiencing repeated or extreme
exposure to aversive details of traumatic events.

### Criterion B — Intrusion symptoms (one or more required)
Recurrent involuntary intrusive memories; recurrent distressing dreams related to the
event; dissociative flashbacks; intense psychological distress at exposure to cues;
marked physiological reactions to cues.

### Criterion C — Avoidance (one or more required)
Persistent avoidance of trauma-related thoughts, feelings, or memories; or avoidance of
external reminders (people, places, situations) that arouse those memories.

### Criterion D — Negative alterations in cognition and mood (two or more required)
Inability to remember key features of the event; persistent exaggerated negative beliefs
about oneself or the world; distorted blame of self or others; persistent negative
emotional state; markedly diminished interest in activities; feelings of detachment;
persistent inability to experience positive emotions.

### Criterion E — Alterations in arousal and reactivity (two or more required)
Irritable behavior or angry outbursts; reckless or self-destructive behavior;
hypervigilance; exaggerated startle response; problems with concentration; sleep
disturbance.

### Duration requirement
The disturbance (Criteria B through E) must last for more than one month. Symptoms
present for less than one month after a trauma may indicate acute stress disorder rather
than PTSD.

### Distress and exclusion criteria
The disturbance causes clinically significant distress or impairment, and is not
attributable to the effects of a substance or another medical condition.

## Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

### Core definition
Excessive anxiety and worry, occurring more days than not for at least six months, about
a number of events or activities. The individual finds it difficult to control the worry.

### Symptom criteria (three or more required in adults)
1. Restlessness or feeling keyed up or on edge.
2. Being easily fatigued.
3. Difficulty concentrating or mind going blank.
4. Irritability.
5. Muscle tension.
6. Sleep disturbance (difficulty falling or staying asleep, or restless sleep).

### Duration requirement
Excessive worry must be present more days than not for at least six months.

### Distress and exclusion criteria
The anxiety causes clinically significant distress or impairment, is not attributable to
a substance or medical condition, and is not better explained by another mental disorder.

## PHQ-8 Severity Banding (screening reference)

The PHQ-8 sums eight items, each scored 0 to 3, for a total of 0 to 24. Note that the
PHQ-8 does NOT include the ninth PHQ-9 item on suicidal ideation, so it cannot be used to
assess crisis or self-harm risk. Standard severity bands map to the four TAO tiers:
- Score 0 to 4: minimal (no or minimal depressive symptoms).
- Score 5 to 9: mild.
- Score 10 to 14: moderate.
- Score 15 to 24: severe (covers moderately severe and severe).
